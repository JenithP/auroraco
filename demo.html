Nine shoppable worlds, one controlled experiment

Aurora & Co. is a fully functional e-commerce store, built from scratch as a research stimulus. Behind the same storefront sit nine independently addressable versions — a 3 × 3 design that crosses what goes wrong at checkout with how the AI service avatar responds to it. Every version is a real, clickable site with identical products, pricing and layout, so any difference we measure comes from the manipulation alone.

3 service-failure conditions 3 avatar personas 9 live versions 17 tracked behavioural events Between-subjects · URL-assigned
01 — The study

What happens to trust when the service breaks?

Participants arrive from an external survey panel, shop normally, and check out. At that moment the experience either runs cleanly or fails in a controlled way — and an AI avatar steps in. We observe what they do next.

Context

A real shopping task

Browse, wishlist, add to cart, adjust quantities, pay from a store credit balance. Nothing is mocked — participants complete a genuine purchase task before anything unusual happens.

Manipulation

A failure, then an avatar

At checkout the site either works, stalls, or overcharges by 10×. In every case a video avatar appears and opens a chat — the same script, three different emotional presentations.

Measurement

Behaviour, not just self-report

Every click, cart change, chatbot message and quick reply is timestamped and streamed to a research spreadsheet, tagged with the version the participant was assigned to.

02 — The design

Two independent axes, assigned by URL

Condition assignment is carried in the link itself — ?c= sets the failure type, ?a= sets the avatar. The panel provider randomises participants across the nine links, so no server-side assignment logic is needed and every cell stays balanced.

Axis A — Service failure

parameter ?c= · between-subjects
c=nm

No failure

  • Checkout completes correctly, order code issued
  • Avatar appears immediately after payment
  • Baseline for the avatar comparison
c=se

Billing error

  • The account is charged 10× the cart total; credit can go negative
  • Avatar appears 5 s later and acknowledges the discrepancy
  • The chat ends unresolved — “2–3 business days to review”
c=dl

System delay

  • The cart is blocked by a “please do not refresh” overlay
  • Avatar appears after 5 s and diagnoses the server
  • The chat resolves the failure; checkout then completes

Axis B — Avatar persona

parameter ?a= · identical dialogue, different delivery

Neutral

a=ne
Composed, professional, low affect. The control persona.

Smiling

a=sm
Warm and positive throughout — including while delivering bad news.

Empathic

a=em
Concerned, affect matched to the participant's situation.

The opening welcome avatar is held constant across all nine versions. The persona manipulation is introduced only at the moment of the service encounter — a full-screen avatar animation (5 s) followed by the matching in-chat video profile — so first impressions cannot confound the recovery effect.

03 — The versions

All nine live sites

Open any cell to experience exactly what a participant in that condition sees. Each link opens in a new tab and behaves as a fresh session; use the copy button to share a single condition with a colleague.

Note for reviewers: these are live instrumented builds — opening a version writes events to the research log under its own session ID. Demo sessions are identifiable by session ID and are excluded from analysis.

04 — The data

What every session produces

Each event is written in real time to a research spreadsheet with session ID, wall-clock time, elapsed seconds since page entry, and the version tag — giving a complete, replayable behavioural trace per participant.

Event Captured detail
PAGE_ENTER Session start and assigned failure condition
PRODUCT_CLICK Product opened, with name and price
WISHLIST_ADD / _REMOVE Wishlist changes
CART_ADD / _QTY_CHANGE / _REMOVE Every basket composition change
CART_OPEN Cart opened — flagged when blocked by the delay overlay
CHECKOUT_CLICK Item count, total and full product list at checkout
PAYMENT_ATTEMPT Billing-error condition: amount charged vs. amount expected
PAYMENT_SUCCESS Order code, condition, amount, items purchased
CHATBOT_AUTO_APPEAR Avatar intervention and the mode that triggered it
CHATBOT_OPEN Participant opened the chat on their own initiative
CHATBOT_USER_MSG Free-text messages typed by the participant
CHATBOT_BOT_MSG Every line the avatar delivered
CHATBOT_QUICK_REPLY Which suggested reply was chosen — the key diagnosis measure
CHATBOT_ISSUE_RESOLVED Recovery completed (delay condition)

Participants finish by returning to the survey platform with an order code generated in session, which links the behavioural trace to their questionnaire responses.
